Guide I

Watch Complications Explained

In watchmaking, a complication is anything a watch does beyond telling the hours, minutes and seconds. Some are everyday helpers; others are feats of engineering.

  • 5 chapters
  • 7 min read
  • Reviewed Nov 2026
✦

I

Everyday complications

The most common is the date, shown through a small window. A day-date adds the day of the week. A power reserve indicator shows how much energy is left in the mainspring, so you know when to wind. These are simple, practical and easy to live with.

✦

II

The moon phase

A moon phase display shows the moon waxing and waning across a small arched window.

  • A standard 59-tooth disc drifts by a day every two and a half years.
  • A high-precision disc can stay accurate for over a century.
  • To set it, find the date of the last full moon and advance the disc from there.
✦

III

Chronograph

A chronograph is a stopwatch built into a watch. Two pushers start, stop and reset a seconds hand, while small subdials count minutes and hours. It is useful for timing anything from a boiled egg to a parking meter.

✦

IV

GMT and world time

A GMT watch adds a fourth hand that turns once every 24 hours, pointing to a second time zone on a 24-hour bezel. A world timer shows the time in all major time zones at once, using a ring of city names.

✦

V

Grand complications

The most difficult include the perpetual calendar, which knows the length of every month and leap year; the minute repeater, which chimes the time on tiny gongs; and the tourbillon, a rotating cage for the escapement. Each can take a skilled watchmaker months to assemble.
